Case Study · 05 · UX

From seeing
to doing

A training platform where HR managers could see compliance data but could not act on it. I designed the solution: a course overview, bulk actions, a course-level detail view and an individual record, structured so the interface moves a manager from monitoring to follow-up without losing their place.

The problem

Data without a next step

The information existed. What was missing was a route through it: a manager could learn that something was wrong without being able to do anything about it from where they stood.

So the work was structural rather than visual. The product's existing look was kept; what changed was the order of the screens and what each one lets you do.

Compliant

Finished and within the validity window. Needs nothing, so it takes the least visual weight.

At risk

Approaching a deadline. The only status that is genuinely time-sensitive, and the reason the overview exists at all.

Non-compliant

Missed or expired. Always paired with the action that resolves it, never shown on its own.

The structure

Four levels, one direction

Each screen answers a narrower question than the one before it, and every level can act, so nobody has to go back up to get something done.

Level 1
All courses. Is anything wrong, and where?
Level 2
Several courses. Fix the same problem in more than one place at once.
Level 3
One course. Who specifically, and what do they need?
Level 4
One person. Everything assigned to them, in one place.
01

Course progress overview

Track completion, compliance status and overall progress on a single course, then act without leaving the view.

02

Multi-course action

Select several courses at once and run a single action across all of them.

03

Course details and progress

Open a course to read status per employee, and follow up from the same screen.

04

Individual view

See one person's status and progress across every training assigned to them.

Outcome

Monitoring that ends in action

Status is legible at a glance, and every place a problem appears is a place it can be resolved. Bulk actions absorb the repetitive part of the job, and the individual view answers the question a manager is asked rather than the one the database was built around.
