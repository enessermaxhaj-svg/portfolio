A landing page
built to ship

A Chrome extension that reads a profile, verifies the work email and drafts the opener, leaving the send to the person. I owned the page end to end: positioning, copy, interface, motion and the front-end build, delivered as a single production file that goes live without a rebuild.

The problem

Selling an approval product

Most tools in this category sell speed, and the market has learned to hear that as risk. The value here runs the other way: the machine does the reading, verifying and drafting, and the person keeps the last click. That distinction had to land in the first screen, because by paragraph four the reader has already decided what kind of product this is.

So the hero shows the artifact being approved rather than an illustration of the idea, and every section below repeats that order: show the thing, then name the constraint that makes it safe. Consistency of argument does more for trust than any single section can.

Positioning line

Autopilot on the work, manual on the judgment. One sentence that separates the product from every send-it-for-you tool on the market, and the line the rest of the page is sequenced around.

The build

Section by section

Each section below is the delivered build itself, embedded live rather than captured as an image. They load as you reach them and stay interactive, so what you are judging is the real page at its real width, not a flattering crop of it.

01

Capture

The first proof: what it found on this person, and where it came from.

Product UIMicro-interaction
Decision

Fields that come back empty are labelled not found rather than hidden. Concealing the gaps would make the whole panel feel staged, and for a product asking to be trusted with someone's inbox, a visible limit does more for credibility than a full grid.

02

Approve

Where the judgment stays with the person: score, claim check, then send.

Core sectionTrust
Decision

The readiness score and claim check sit on the draft itself rather than in a separate panel. A safeguard shown away from the point of risk is a safeguard nobody uses, and moving it here was the difference between a feature and an actual control.

03

How it works

Read, verify, draft. The send is held back on purpose.

ExplainerHierarchy
Decision

The held-back send is drawn as a deliberate stop rather than left out of the sequence. When the absence of a step is the product's main claim, it has to be visible, or the reader has to take it on faith.

04

The package

Dossier, message and cadence, generated and reviewed as one thing.

Product proofPricing support
Decision

It is generated together, reviewed together and billed together, so presenting it as three separate features would have described the build rather than the experience. This also let the pricing section stay short, which is where short belongs.

05

Features

Constraints written as capabilities, because that is what buyers are shopping for.

GridPositioning
Decision

Each card names the constraint before the capability it enables. It is the same argument the hero makes, repeated at feature scale, which is how a page stays consistent with itself rather than merely well designed in parts.

06

Pricing and FAQ

Free on the first campaign, with the objections answered directly underneath.

ConversionClose
Decision

The FAQ sits immediately below the price rather than at the foot of the page. Objections are cheapest to answer while the number is still on screen, and moving them here measurably shortens the distance between interest and a click.

Handover

Delivered deployable

One HTML file. No framework, no build step, no dependency to install. Semantic sections with stable ids, scoped class names, and all motion behind prefers-reduced-motion. Every animation is canvas or CSS, driven by an IntersectionObserver so nothing off screen is running.

The practical effect is that there is no handoff gap to manage. No redline pass, no second round on spacing, no engineer rebuilding a static frame in a different stack. What was designed is what deploys, which removes the stage where most of the intent usually leaks out.
